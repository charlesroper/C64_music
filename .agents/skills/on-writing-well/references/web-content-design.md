# Web and content design

Apply this playbook when the active Surface is **Web & Content Design**: web pages, landing pages, help-centre articles, service pages, onboarding hubs, FAQs and public guidance.

## 1. Anchor to a user need (Winters)

Every web page exists to satisfy a specific **Reader Job**. Before drafting, frame the user need as a job story:

> **When I** [situation], **I want to** [motivation or task], **so I can** [outcome].

- **Include only what serves the job:** If a paragraph exists to explain internal team structure, company history or policy background that the reader does not need to complete their task, cut it.
- **Put conditions and eligibility first:** State who a service, feature or rule applies to (and any prerequisites, costs or limits) at the top of the page before the reader invests time reading the steps.

## 2. Design for foraging (Redish)

Readers on screens forage in an F-pattern, scanning headings, bold triggers and first lines to decide in seconds whether a page answers their question.

- **Structure as Bite, Snack, Meal:**
  - **Bite (Title and opening summary):** Answer the core question or state the core value proposition in the H1 and the first one to two sentences.
  - **Snack (Subheadings, callouts and lists):** Let a skimming reader grasp the whole process, comparison or decision by reading only the H2/H3 headings and bulleted lists.
  - **Meal (Body paragraphs and expandable detail):** Provide the nuance, edge cases and supporting detail beneath each subheading for readers who need depth.
- **Keep paragraphs short for screens:** Cap web paragraphs at three to four sentences. One-sentence paragraphs are welcome when landing a key condition or call to action.
- **Write question-answering headings:** Frame headings around the user's task or question (`## Check if your device is compatible` or `## How billing cycles work`), never generic labels (`## Overview` or `## Details`).

## 3. Plain language and vocabulary alignment (Winters)

- **Use the reader's search vocabulary:** Name concepts using the everyday words your audience types into a search bar, replacing internal product code names and departmental jargon. When introducing a specialist term, pair it with the plain term in the heading.
- **Turn `if/then` prose into tables or lists:** Whenever a sentence contains more than two conditions (*"If you are on the Pro plan and signed up before May, unless you use SSO..."*), break it into a bulleted decision list or a lookup table.
- **Write self-describing links:** Make link text name the destination or action (`Read the API rate limits`, never `Click here` or `Learn more`).

### Before and after: foraging and conditional rules

**Before:**
> Welcome to our account management portal. We are committed to providing flexible subscription options for all of our customers. Please note that if you wish to downgrade your workspace plan, adjustments take effect at the conclusion of your current billing period for monthly subscribers, whereas annual subscribers who downgrade more than thirty days after renewal will receive prorated account credits rather than a cash refund, unless the account is registered in the EU. For more information, click here.

**After:**
> ## Change or downgrade your plan
>
> You can downgrade your workspace plan at any time from **Billing settings**. When the change takes effect depends on your billing cycle:
>
> - **Monthly plans:** Your downgrade starts at the end of the current billing month.
> - **Annual plans (within 30 days of renewal):** You receive a full refund for the unused difference.
> - **Annual plans (after 30 days):** You receive prorated account credit, or a prorated cash refund if your billing address is in the EU or UK.
>
> [Open Billing settings](/settings/billing)

## Pre-delivery checklist

1. Can a reader tell what the page answers and whether it applies to them within five seconds?
2. Do the headings and bulleted lists alone communicate the core path (**Bite, Snack, Meal**)?
3. Are complex `if/then` conditions formatted as lists or tables rather than dense prose?
4. Do all links describe their destination without using "click here" or "learn more"?
5. Are internal jargon terms replaced with the words the audience actually uses?
